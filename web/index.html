Two dialects, one engine: ChemSage .dat (the FactSage / ChemApp / Thermochimica family; the only format that carries the MQMQA melt model, so slags and salts live here) and Thermo-Calc .tdb (the Thermo-Calc / OpenCalphad / pycalphad family; alloy solid-solution systems). The .utdb unified dialect, a documented open extension of TDB, holds both at once: one file can carry an alloy, a slag, and a salt together (see the unified demo). Uploaded .tdb/.utdb files pin in teal/pink, .dat files in purple/blue; up to three at once, either mix.
Uploaded databases are temporary — they live only in this tab and vanish when you refresh. Up to three at once.

Phase diagram builder · build & tune your ownyour data

Enter each component's own data — melting point and heat of fusion, plus a single liquid interaction energy W — and the engine computes the eutectic live from multicomponent regular-solution thermodynamics, for up to four components, solved in your browser. Change any value (the sliders are quickest) and watch the diagram and the eutectic move. Load a preset to start, or enter a brand-new system.

One symmetric W applies to every pair (a first approximation). W < 0 (components attract): a deeper eutectic — the deep-eutectic-solvent regime; strongly negative → compound formation. W > 0 (components repel): a shallower eutectic that rises toward liquid immiscibility as W grows.
 
If you build with your own data, read this. This is a regular-solution model with a single symmetric interaction: it turns your melting points, heats of fusion, and W into a diagram, but it is an approximation and the result is only as good as the numbers you enter. Treat a diagram you built as a starting hypothesis to check against your own data and judgment — it does not replace a proper thermodynamic assessment. For three or more components the eutectic is a numerical estimate over the composition simplex, and one symmetric W will not reproduce a strongly-ordered real system exactly (per-pair interactions and compounds are the assessed-engine path). An unexpected diagram is usually the input data (or a typo), not the engine. Stuck, or think something is off? Email us at info@odinzen.io and we will help you sort it out.

Request a database for your systemconcierge

Have a stack of published papers on a system the open database does not cover yet, whether your own work or literature you have gathered (phase-diagram studies, calorimetry, activity measurements)? That is what an assessed database is built from, the way the open slag database above was made: measured values extracted with provenance, an excess model fitted by this same engine, and a valid database file out the other end — ChemSage .dat when the system needs the MQMQA melt (slags, salts), or Thermo-Calc .tdb for alloy solid-solution systems. This page reads and calculates both.

Published data, opened. No coding is involved at any point: you send papers, and what comes back is a working button on this page or a quoted proposal. If a system is a bounded, natural addition to the open database, we may assess it and add it here as another open database, free, and send you a starter file in the dialect you work in. Sources are cited to their original authors; the contribution is credited to whoever brought it. That covers low-maintenance growth of the commons, and light ongoing additions are welcome. It is not a blanket free service, though: open additions are taken a few at a time as capacity allows, and a large or long-running assessment, or heavy ongoing upkeep, is paid work. We open the science, but sustaining it needs a work product on our side once the scope grows past a small system.

Proprietary or confidential data is assessed privately and the file stays yours, as a paid service. Either way, the same address: point this card at your paper folder to list the papers, then email info@odinzen.io. Your files are listed locally in your browser and never uploaded, there is no server behind this page.
